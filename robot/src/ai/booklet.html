[% self.system.name() %]: the bidding system

What each call means in every auction the system has an agreement about, starting from the opening bid. This is written from the same table the robots bid by, so it is exactly what they will do and what they will take your bids to mean.

How to read this

There is a chapter for each kind of opening bid, with a page for each stage of the auction after it: the responses, opener's rebid and so on. A page is a table of auctions, lowest first. The last call of each auction is in bold, and the rest of the line is what that call means and the hand it shows.

Auctions that mean the same are written together:

X, Y, Z, W
Any suit that can be bid there. Different letters are different suits, and a letter is never a suit that the same auction names. 1X – 1Y – 2Y is 1♣ – 1♥ – 2♥, or 1♦ – 1♠ – 2♠, and so on, and in 1X – 2♣ the opening is anything but 1♣.
M, m
A major suit (♥ or ♠), and a minor suit (♣ or ♦).
2♦/♥/♠
2♦, 2♥ or 2♠.
1X – 1Y – 1NT/2X – 2NT
Either call: this is 1X – 1Y – 1NT – 2NT and 1X – 1Y – 2X – 2NT.
(1♠), (Dbl), (Pass)
A call in parentheses is an opponent's. When the opponents have only passed, their passes are left out.
or over (Dbl)
The call means the same when the opponent before you doubles instead of passing.
(2Z: Reverse)
A note after an auction says which of its auctions are meant: here, only those where 2Z was a reverse. (opened in third seat) is for auctions that two passes came before.
The suit bid
The suit of the call in bold. "Your suit" and "partner's suit" are the suits you and partner bid earlier in the auction, and "their suit" is the opponents'.

For a call that is on no page, see Bids that no page describes.

High card points
Ace 4, king 3, queen 2, jack 1.
Long high card points
High card points, plus one for each card beyond four in a suit.
Short high card points, or dummy points
For a hand that will be dummy in a suit contract. Add 3 for a void, 2 for a singleton and 1 for a doubleton, but count less for honours in short suits: a singleton king is 1 and a singleton queen 0, a king or queen in a doubleton is one less than usual, and a jack counts only in a suit of three or more.
Losers
The New Losing Trick Count. In the top three cards of each suit, a missing ace is 1½ losers, a missing king 1 and a missing queen ½, counting no more cards than the suit has. Fewer losers is a stronger hand.
forcing
Partner may not pass.
game forcing
Neither partner may pass until game is reached.
